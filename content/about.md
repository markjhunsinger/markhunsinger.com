I'm a penetration tester based in northeast Pennsylvania.

My work covers external and internal network testing, web applications, and
social engineering, along with some onsite physical assessment work covering
WiFi, Bluetooth, NFC, and rogue USB devices. I've also built a handful of
internal tools that automate parts of the testing workflow and cut down the
tedium in reporting.

I came into security sideways. I spent years in customer service and IT support
before moving into cloud and security engineering, and then into pentesting.
That path turned out to be more useful than expected, since a lot of this job is
sitting with clients and explaining what happened without making anyone feel
stupid about it.

Lately I've been spending more time on the adversary emulation side of things. A
pentest tells you what can be broken. A red team engagement tells you whether
anyone notices, how long it takes, and what they do about it. That second
question is the one I find more interesting, and it's the direction I've been
building toward.
